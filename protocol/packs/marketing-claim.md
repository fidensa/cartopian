# Marketing Claim Practice Pack

Use this optional guidance only for the selected marketing outcome. It does not change the task risk, activate judgment guidance, alter configured review policy, or create a universal gate. Wherever a narrower current authority governs — the jurisdiction's law and regulator guidance, a platform's advertising policy, the operator's approved brand and legal authority, the operator's task contract — that authority overrides this pack inside its scope.

## Intended Outcome

Deliver a supportable audience-facing marketing outcome: the intended audience and the behavior asked of it defined before drafting, every material claim inventoried and matched to operator-approved brand authority, substantiation held in hand before delivery, disclosures the audience will actually notice, triggered legal review completed under the governing jurisdiction, channel and launch checks run against the final asset, and measurement whose interpreting decision was fixed before results arrived. The evidence this pack produces is claim-to-evidence fit, audience and channel fit, and outcome observation — not impressions, asset volume, or production effort.

## When To Apply

Apply this pack when the task's primary outcome is an audience-facing claim, campaign asset, positioning decision, or channel result: deciding or drafting what an external audience will be told, choosing where and how to tell it, or deciding what a channel result means. Scale depth to consequence: a campaign decision walks the full working process; a single small asset uses the decision gates to establish which claims are material and records why lighter treatment sufficed.

## When Not To Apply

Do not apply when marketing is only the subject or an input: research about marketing effectiveness, policy analysis of advertising rules, software that implements marketing features, or an operational record that mentions a campaign follows its own outcome's guidance. Do not apply to incidental promotional language inside another outcome — product wording in internal documentation or a README follows that work's contract. Do not apply when the task envelope excludes marketing guidance. Selecting this pack never authorizes delivery: campaign launch, external publication, and binding brand commitments keep their own operator approval boundaries regardless of what this guidance finds.

## Principles And Heuristics

Each principle names when it helps, when it may not apply, and the evidence that supports using it. None is a universal gate. Throughout, the ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code, 11th edition (September 2024), supplies the global self-regulatory baseline — communications must be legal, decent, honest, and truthful, and claims must be substantiated — subordinate to applicable law, regulator guidance, and operator authority inside their scopes.

### Intended Audience And Behavior

Name the real audience from evidence — the operator's stated market, existing customer data, the channel's actual reach — plus its decision or need and the single behavior the asset asks for. Helps when the brief assumes a generic customer or "everyone." May not apply when the operator has already fixed audience and objective. Evidence: the audience definition with its supporting fact, and the behavioral outcome the measurement later observes.

### Brand Authority

Brand position, naming, voice, and claims territory are operator-owned. Distinguish established facts, approved positioning, and aspiration; make no new brand promise or positioning move without the named operator authority that approved it. Helps whenever an asset states who the brand is or what it commits to. May not apply to strictly informative content that stays inside already-approved positioning. Evidence: the approval, or the existing approved source, behind each brand-level statement.

### Claim Inventory

Before substantiation, review, or launch, list every material claim the asset makes — express and implied, including comparisons, superlatives, pricing, performance figures, endorsements, and what images or demonstrations suggest. A claim the audience reasonably takes away is a claim, whether or not it was intended. Helps for every audience-facing asset. May not apply when the asset makes no material claim — and that determination is itself the recorded result. Evidence: the inventory that every substantiation, disclosure, and review entry keys to.

### Substantiation

Hold proof before delivery, matched to the claim as the audience takes it: objective claims need competent supporting evidence in hand before dissemination, not assembled after challenge. A test of one configuration does not substantiate a general superiority claim. An unsupported material claim is revised, qualified, or dropped — never published pending proof. Helps for every objective claim. May not apply to obvious non-measurable puffery, which is narrower than convenient. Evidence: the claim-to-evidence map for the inventory's material claims.

### Disclosures

A disclosure the audience does not notice does not exist. Disclose material connections, paid and affiliate relationships, endorsement arrangements, and material conditions clearly, conspicuously, and near the claim they qualify, in the form the channel actually renders. Helps whenever a reasonable audience would weigh the connection or condition. May not apply when there is nothing material to disclose. Evidence: the placement check on the rendered asset, per channel.

### Jurisdictional Legal Review

Resolve which jurisdiction and regulator govern before finalizing claims, then route triggered work to the operator's authorized legal or compliance review: comparative and superiority claims, health, financial, or environmental claims, endorsements, testimonials, and consumer reviews, pricing and offers, data and privacy uses, and audiences that include children are common triggers. For United States work the Federal Trade Commission authority set applies conditionally: FTC Act claim-substantiation authority, the Endorsement Guides as revised 2023, and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule effective 2024-10-21. Outside the United States that set does not govern; resolve that jurisdiction's own law and regulator guidance first. The ICC code remains a self-regulatory baseline, never a substitute for law. Evidence: the named jurisdiction, the triggers checked, and each review's disposition.

### Channel Choice

Choose channels from where the intended audience actually makes its decision and from what each channel can render — format, length, and disclosure placement — not from habit or availability. A platform's advertising policy governs inside that platform. Helps when more than one channel could carry the asset. May not apply when the operator has fixed the channel. Evidence: the recorded channel rationale and the platform-policy check.

### Launch Checks

Verify the final rendered asset, not the draft: claims match the approved inventory, disclosures are present and visible as rendered, prices, dates, and destinations are correct, and every required approval — brand, triggered legal review, and the operator's launch approval — is in hand. This pack never satisfies the launch gate itself. Helps immediately before any delivery. May not apply before then. Evidence: the completed pre-launch check tied to the asset that will actually ship.

### Measurement Design

Define measurement before launch: the outcome metric tied to the intended behavior, the baseline, the audience or channel segment, the observation window, the decision threshold, and the decision each result triggers. Helps whenever a result will justify a next step. May not apply when the operator supplied the measurement plan. Evidence: the plan recorded before launch, with its measurement source.

### Interpretation Limits

State attribution limits and confounds before reading results: concurrent campaigns, seasonality, selection effects, and the gap between proxy metrics and the intended behavior. Clicks and impressions are not the outcome unless the plan declared them so. A threshold is not moved after the results are known. Helps for every measured launch. May not apply when no result will be interpreted. Evidence: the interpretation stated against the predefined threshold, with its limits.

### Follow-Up

Every launch ends in a predefined decision — continue, adjust, stop, or escalate — with a named owner, and a correction path for published errors: who withdraws or corrects the asset, how fast, and through which channel. Helps for every delivered asset. May not apply to work that never delivered. Evidence: the recorded follow-up decision and the named correction owner.

## Working Process

1. Define the intended audience, the behavior asked, and the outcome; confirm brand authority covers the proposition. Stop at the first decisive ambiguity (see Stop And Escalation).
2. Inventory every express and implied material claim in the proposition and asset.
3. Substantiate: match each material claim to evidence held now; revise, qualify, or drop what is unsupported.
4. Resolve the governing jurisdiction, check review triggers, and route triggered legal or compliance review; place required disclosures in the rendered form.
5. Choose the channel against audience and rendering constraints; verify platform policy fit.
6. Predefine measurement: metric, baseline, segment, window, threshold, and the decision each result triggers.
7. Run launch checks on the final rendered asset and confirm every required approval, including the operator's launch approval, is in hand — launch itself stays behind its own gate.
8. After results: interpret against the predefined threshold within the stated limits; record the follow-up decision, the evidence produced, and the remaining gaps.

## Decision Gates

Answer before proceeding past each stage; a no is a stop or an escalation, not a rationalization.

- Can I name the real audience and the single behavior this asset asks for, with the supporting fact?
- Is every claim — express and implied — in the inventory?
- Is the substantiation for each material claim in hand now, matched to the claim as the audience takes it?
- Does the operator's approved brand and legal authority cover every commitment this asset makes?
- Which jurisdiction governs, and has every triggered legal review reached a disposition?
- Would the audience actually notice each required disclosure in this channel's rendered form?
- Is the measurement decision predefined — which result triggers which action, decided before launch?
- Whose approval does launch require — and has anything here been treated as granting it?

## Failure Modes

Each entry pairs the failure with the rationalization that disguises it.

- Invented audience: a persona built from assumption rather than evidence — "we know our customers." An audience definition is a claim; it needs its supporting fact.
- Unapproved brand claim: positioning invented inside an asset — "it's on brand." Brand authority is operator-owned, not inferred from tone.
- Evidence-free superlative: best, fastest, or number one with no proof in hand — "that's just puffery." Implied measurable superiority is a substantiation claim; real puffery is narrower than convenient.
- Buried disclosure: a material connection technically present but effectively invisible — "it's in the footer." A disclosure the audience does not see does not cure the claim.
- Proxy-metric substitution: declaring success on clicks when the declared outcome was behavior — "engagement is up." The proxy was not the promise.
- Vanity metrics: reporting numbers no predefined decision consumes — "the numbers look great." Measurement exists to trigger a decision, not to decorate a report.
- Attribution overclaiming: crediting the campaign for outcomes with confounds unexamined — "sales rose right after launch." After is not because.
- Launch without a follow-up decision: shipping with no predefined continue, adjust, or stop rule — "we'll see how it goes." That defers the decision to the moment with the least objectivity.

## Evidence And Verification

Produce evidence a reviewer can check without trusting the author: the claim inventory with substantiation identified per material claim; the brand and legal approval records with the authority that granted each; the rendered-asset disclosure check per channel; the named jurisdiction with triggers and review dispositions; the pre-launch check against the final asset; the predefined measurement plan; the interpretation against its threshold with stated limits; and the recorded follow-up decision. Claims connect to proof, launch connects to verification, and measurement connects to a predefined decision. Impressions, asset counts, spend, or approval-in-principle are never by themselves evidence.

## Examples And Counterexamples

- Proportional: a release announcement stating shipped facts inside approved positioning needs the claim inventory and a disclosure pass, not the full campaign process — its material claims are the factual statements themselves, and each still traces to what shipped.
- Legal review required: a United States campaign using incentivized customer testimonials triggers the 2023 Endorsement Guides and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule effective 2024-10-21 — disclosure and review-handling checks reach a disposition before launch.
- Counterexample: launching "fastest in its class" with the benchmark planned for later — an evidence-free superlative; substantiation precedes dissemination.
- Counterexample: applying the United States FTC authority set to a campaign running only in Germany — a current source outside its jurisdiction; the governing law and regulator guidance are resolved first, with the ICC code as baseline.
- Counterexample: routing an internal design document that mentions the product's value proposition through this pack — internal documentation with incidental promotional language is not an audience-facing outcome.

## Stop And Escalation

Stop and report rather than proceed when: a material claim cannot be substantiated with evidence in hand; required brand or legal authority is not granted, or a triggered review cannot reach a disposition; the governing jurisdiction for a material claim cannot be resolved; a required disclosure cannot be made effective in the chosen channel; or launch, external publication, or a binding brand commitment would occur without its own operator approval. Escalate with the failure-signal grammar: the unverified claim, the missing authority or evidence, the consequence of proceeding, and the decision or proof required. Hand off with the claim inventory and its substantiation state, the approval and review dispositions, the launch-check state, the measurement plan, and the follow-up decision owner.

# Research Inquiry Practice Pack

Use this optional guidance only for the selected research outcome. It does not change the task risk, activate judgment guidance, alter configured review policy, or create a universal gate. Wherever a narrower current authority governs — an adopted institutional or funder research-integrity code, a field's methodological authority, a governing legal or organizational rule, the operator's task contract — that authority overrides this pack inside its scope.

## Intended Outcome

Deliver a supported finding: a question framed precisely enough to be answered wrongly, evidence gathered by a declared strategy with a declared stop rule, every decisive claim mapped to identified support, conflicts and uncertainty carried visibly instead of averaged away, and a synthesis another reader can verify from the citations alone. The evidence this pack produces is source provenance, claim support, uncertainty, and contradiction — not reading effort, source counts, or citation volume.

## When To Apply

Apply this pack when the task's primary outcome is a supported finding, comparison, synthesis, or source-based answer: evaluating competing claims, comparing options against evidence, synthesizing what multiple sources establish, or answering a question whose answer must survive challenge. Scale depth to consequence and decisiveness: a finding that drives a material decision walks the full working process; a modest finding uses the decision gates to establish which claims are decisive and records why lighter treatment sufficed.

## When Not To Apply

Do not apply to pure lookup — retrieving a single value from one authoritative source, where the whole method is confirming the source's identity and version and citing it. Do not apply when source reading is incidental to another primary outcome: reading documentation to implement a change, checking a fact inside a campaign asset, or consulting a runbook during an executed procedure follows that outcome's guidance, not this pack. Subject matter never selects guidance by itself: a task that merely mentions studies, sources, or research follows its own declared outcome. Do not apply when the task envelope excludes research guidance.

## Principles And Heuristics

Each principle names when it helps, when it may not apply, and the evidence that supports using it. None is a universal gate. Throughout, the ALLEA European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity (revised edition 2023) supplies the integrity baseline — reliability, honesty, respect, and accountability — with governing force only where it is adopted or required; narrower field, institutional, or jurisdictional codes govern inside their own scopes.

### Question Framing And Scope

State the question so it can be answered wrongly: the claim under test, the comparison frame, what is inside and outside scope, and the decision the finding informs. Helps when the request arrives as a topic rather than a question. May not apply when the operator has already fixed the question and scope. Evidence: the question statement the synthesis later answers, with its inclusion and exclusion rules.

### Source Strategy And Hierarchy

Plan the search before searching: which channels, which query terms, and what would count as authoritative for this claim. Prefer sources with direct authority over the claim — the standard's publisher for what the standard says, the primary dataset for what was measured — and record why a weaker source was accepted when a stronger one was unavailable. Helps whenever more than one source could speak to a claim. May not apply when a governing contract names the authoritative source. Evidence: the recorded strategy, the queries run, and the authority rationale per decisive source.

### Primary Versus Secondary Evidence

Distinguish evidence from commentary about evidence. A decisive claim traces to the primary record — the original study, measurement, ruling, or document — with secondary sources used as leads and context, not as termini. Helps when claims arrive pre-interpreted. May not apply when the primary record is inaccessible and the limitation is recorded. Evidence: the provenance chain from each decisive claim to its most primary available support.

### Independence And Triangulation

Two sources repeating one origin are one source. Before counting agreement as corroboration, check whether the sources share an origin, an author, a dataset, or an incentive. Corroborate decisive claims across genuinely independent origins. Helps for contested or surprising claims. May not apply to claims a single controlling authority settles. Evidence: the independence note attached to each corroboration.

### Freshness And Version Context

Record each source's publication, effective, edition, or version context and verify the claim against the version that governs the question's timeframe. A current source is not universally applicable, and an older source is not automatically wrong — supersession is checked, not assumed. Helps for standards, prices, laws, APIs, and anything versioned. May not apply to settled historical fact. Evidence: the recorded date or version per decisive source and the supersession check for each.

### Method Fit

Match the method to the question: a systematic comparison, a targeted verification, and an exploratory survey answer different questions at different cost. Method-specific authority binds only inside its scope: PRISMA 2020 and its applicable official extensions govern reporting when the work is a systematic review or an applicable evidence-synthesis type; the Cochrane Handbook 6.5.1 governs methods when the work is a systematic review of healthcare interventions. Outside those scopes they do not govern, and the field's own applicable authority is identified instead. Evidence: the named method, why it fits the question, and which method authority — if any — applies.

### Claim-Evidence Mapping

Map every decisive claim to the specific evidence that supports it, by identity — source, location, version — not by vibe. A claim without support is marked unsupported, never silently blended into supported text. Helps as the backbone of any finding. May not apply to incidental background statements no decision rests on. Evidence: the map a reviewer can walk from each decisive claim to its support.

### Conflicts And Contradictions

When sources disagree, resolve the conflict through an explicit rule — scope, authority, recency, or method quality — and record both sides and the disposition. An unresolved conflict on a decisive claim is a recorded blocker, not an average or a coin flip. Helps whenever evidence points both ways. May not apply when the conflict touches nothing decisive. Evidence: the conflict record with its rule and disposition.

### Uncertainty

State what remains unknown: missing evidence, untestable assumptions, method limits, and the consequence of each for the finding. Express uncertainty by naming the missing evidence and what would resolve it, not by inventing a confidence number. Helps in every nontrivial finding. May not apply when every decisive claim is fully verified. Evidence: the recorded assumptions, limits, and their consequences.

### Synthesis

Conclusions follow from the mapped evidence and nothing else. Separate observation from interpretation, consider the strongest alternative explanation before adopting one, and keep the finding's strength proportional to its support. Helps when many sources must become one answer. May not apply to a single-claim verification. Evidence: a synthesis whose every load-bearing statement traces into the claim-evidence map.

### Citation And Fact-Checking

Cite the exact source consulted — edition, version, date, location — so a reader lands on what was actually read. Before delivery, verify quotations, dates, quantities, definitions, and version context against the sources themselves. Helps for everything delivered onward. May not apply to internal scratch notes. Evidence: resolvable citations and the recorded verification pass over decisive facts.

### Search And Stopping Rules

Declare the stop condition before searching: saturation (new searches return already-seen sources), coverage (every decisive claim has support or a recorded gap), or a bounded effort with the remaining gaps named. Stopping is a decision with evidence, and continuing past the rule needs a named gap, not unease. Helps to keep inquiry proportional. May not apply to a bounded lookup inside a larger task. Evidence: the declared rule and the disposition that ended the search.

## Working Process

1. Frame the question, scope, and the decision it informs. Stop at the first decisive ambiguity (see Stop And Escalation).
2. Declare the search strategy and the stop rule before gathering.
3. Gather sources, recording provenance as you go: identity, authorship, publication or version context, and where each was found.
4. Classify support — primary or secondary, independent or shared-origin — and verify freshness and version context for decisive sources.
5. Map claims to evidence; mark unsupported claims, conflicts, and gaps as they appear rather than at the end.
6. Resolve conflicts by an explicit rule or record them unresolved; fact-check quotations, dates, quantities, and definitions on decisive claims.
7. Synthesize proportionally: state the finding, its support, the alternatives considered, and the remaining uncertainty.
8. Record evidence and gaps: queries run, sources consulted and excluded with reasons, unverified claims with their consequence, and the next proof each gap requires.

## Decision Gates

Answer before proceeding past each stage; a no is a stop or an escalation, not a rationalization.

- Could this question be answered wrongly — and do I know what evidence would change the answer?
- Is the declared search strategy recorded, with a stop rule I could be held to?
- Does every decisive claim trace to its most primary available source, with provenance recorded?
- Are the corroborating sources actually independent of one another?
- Is the date, edition, or version context verified for every decisive source, in the timeframe the question governs?
- Does a method-specific authority govern this inquiry — and am I inside its declared scope before applying it?
- Has any decisive conflict been resolved by a named rule, or recorded as unresolved?
- Has the stop rule been reached — and if I keep searching, what named gap justifies it?

## Failure Modes

Each entry pairs the failure with the rationalization that disguises it.

- Search-result substitution: treating whatever the search surfaced as the evidence base — "this is what's out there." Coverage is a claim that needs its own evidence: the strategy, the channels, and the stop rule.
- Citation laundering: a secondary source citing a secondary source until the origin disappears — "it's widely cited." Popularity of a citation is not provenance; trace it to origin or mark it unverified.
- False corroboration: counting dependent repetitions as agreement — "multiple sources confirm this." Shared origin makes them one source.
- Stale evidence: the right source in the wrong version or timeframe — "it was true when published." Decisive claims are verified against the version that governs the question.
- Cherry-picking: keeping supportive evidence and discarding the rest — "the exceptions are edge cases." Inclusion rules are declared before gathering, not fitted to the conclusion.
- Unsupported synthesis: a conclusion exceeding its mapped support — "it stands to reason." A finding is as strong as its weakest decisive claim.
- Endless searching: gathering past the stop rule without a named gap — "one more search to be safe." Unbounded search is avoidance of a decision, not rigor.

## Evidence And Verification

Produce evidence a reviewer can check without trusting the author: the recorded search strategy, queries, and channels; a provenance record for every decisive source; the claim-evidence map; independence notes on corroborated claims; conflict records with rule and disposition; unverified claims with decisiveness, consequence, and next proof; and the stop-rule disposition. Reading effort, the number of sources consulted, and the length of the bibliography are never by themselves evidence that a finding is supported.

## Examples And Counterexamples

- Proportional: a comparison of three vendors' license terms cites each vendor's current license text with version and retrieval date, marks one term unverifiable from public sources as a non-decisive gap, and stops at coverage. It does not need a systematic-review protocol.
- Same subject, different methods: two inquiries about one medication — a synthesis of intervention trials falls under Cochrane Handbook 6.5.1 methods and PRISMA 2020 reporting; a market-availability question about the same medication falls under neither, and applying their checklists to it is a scope error, not extra rigor.
- Counterexample: a finding comparing two frameworks' security models is research even though its subject is software — the outcome is a supported finding, so software mentioned only as the research subject does not select software guidance.
- Counterexample: five articles all restating one benchmark presented as five corroborating sources — false corroboration with an empty independence column.
- Counterexample: routing a one-line factual lookup through this whole pack — a bounded lookup needed only source identity, version, and a citation.

## Stop And Escalation

Stop searching when the declared stop rule is met. Stop and report rather than proceed when: a decisive claim cannot be verified with the sources and access available; decisive sources conflict and no declared rule or authority resolves them; the question requires a method, expertise, or access outside the assignment; or the freshness or version context of a decisive source cannot be established. Escalate with the failure-signal grammar: the unverified claim, the missing authority or evidence, the consequence of proceeding, and the decision or proof required. Hand off with the question and scope, the claim-evidence map, the sources with provenance, the open gaps, and the next proof each gap requires.

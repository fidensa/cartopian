# Policy Governance Practice Pack

Use this optional guidance only for the selected policy outcome. It does not change the task risk, grant authority, activate judgment guidance, alter configured review policy, or create a universal gate. Wherever a narrower current authority governs — applicable law, the regulator, the organization's delegated authority, an existing instrument, the operator's task contract — that authority overrides this pack inside its scope.

## Intended Outcome

Deliver a governing interpretation, rule, or governance position that can be relied on: authority named before drafting, objective stated, participation run where owed, impact supported by evidence including who bears the cost, conflicts resolved, the decision made by its holder, the text published where its audience can reach it, dates that answer "which rule applied when," implementation owned, and monitoring and review set before it takes effect. The evidence is authority, applicability, interpretation, effect, and exception — not consultation volume or the fact that a document was approved.

## When To Apply

Apply this pack when the task's primary outcome is an interpretation, rule, governance position, compliance effect, or decision under stated authority: drafting or amending a policy, standard, or procedure that binds someone; issuing an interpretation that controls a downstream decision; deciding an exception; or publishing, dating, or withdrawing controlling text. Scale depth to consequence: a binding rule with external effect walks the full process; a narrow clarification inside settled authority uses the gates to establish which steps are decisive and why the rest were not required.

## When Not To Apply

Do not apply when policy is only the subject or an input: research about a regulation, marketing describing a compliance feature, software implementing a rule, or an operational record citing a procedure follows its own outcome's guidance. Do not apply to ordinary prose that binds no one — a README, a runbook, notes, internal preference, or a recommendation nobody must follow — beyond confirming it states no rule and creates no obligation. Do not apply when the envelope excludes policy guidance. Selecting this pack never authorizes issuance: adopting, publishing, or interpreting on the record stays behind its own operator and legal approval.

## Principles And Heuristics

Each principle names what it asks for, where it does not apply, and the evidence it produces. None is a universal gate. Throughout, the OECD Recommendation on Regulatory Policy and Governance (OECD/LEGAL/0390, adopted 2012-03-22) is the foundational baseline — explicit objectives, participation, evidence-based impact assessment, coherence across instruments, systematic review — subordinate to applicable law, the regulator, and the organization's own decision authority.

### Governing Authority And Jurisdiction

Name the instrument, statute, delegation, or charter that lets this text bind anyone, and the jurisdiction, entities, activities, and period it reaches; an interpretation inherits the scope of the authority it construes and can never widen it. Evidence: the cited authority with its scope and the named decision holder.

### Problem And Objective

State the problem in observable terms, who experiences it, and the outcome intended, separately from the mechanism chosen; an objective of "have a policy" cannot be evaluated later. May not apply to a conforming amendment matching a superior instrument. Evidence: the objective statement monitoring later measures against.

### Stakeholder Participation

Consult affected stakeholders, implementers, and those bearing material consequences early enough that the outcome can still change, in a form they can use, and record what was raised and its disposition. Consultation is evidence, not decision authority; a mandatory procedure set by the governing instrument governs instead. Evidence: the participation record.

### Evidence And Impact

Support each expected effect proportionately to consequence: baseline, expected change, compliance cost, and who bears it. Where impact assessment is the work, the OECD Regulatory Impact Assessment Best Practice Principles (2020) inform its design and proportionality; outside impact assessment they are not general policy authority. Evidence: each claimed effect mapped to its supporting fact.

### Alternatives

Compare the realistic alternatives — doing nothing, guidance instead of a rule, an instrument already covering the ground — and record why the chosen mechanism fits. For innovation, experimentation, or pilots, OECD/LEGAL/0464 (2021) conditionally informs the design and does not govern outside regulatory innovation and experimentation. Evidence: the comparison and fit rationale.

### Conflicts And Distributional Effects

Check the text against existing instruments, prior interpretations, contracts, and superior authority, and resolve each conflict by precedence, narrowing, or amendment; unresolved same-scope conflict is a stop. Assess distributional effects: which groups gain, which bear cost, who can least absorb it. Where obligations cross borders, OECD/LEGAL/0475 (2022) conditionally informs transboundary co-operation and does not reach domestic-only work. Evidence: the conflict list with dispositions and the distributional finding.

### Compliance

Map every material requirement to its controlling authority, scope, interpretation, evidence of conformance, and exception path; distinguish binding requirements from guidance, and state what non-conformance triggers and who determines it. Evidence: the requirement-to-authority map with its verification and exception routes.

### Decision Ownership

Name the person or body that decides, the delegation granting it, the approval form required, and what an unresolved objection triggers; drafting, consulting, and reviewing are not deciding. Evidence: the decision record with authority, date, rationale, and why alternatives were rejected.

### Publication

Publish the approved text through the authoritative publisher, to the audience it binds, in a channel and format they can reach and use, with a version identity separating it from drafts and superseded editions; text the bound audience cannot obtain or read has not been published to them. Evidence: the published location, version identity, notification, and proof no draft is reachable as authoritative.

### Effective Dates

Record approval, publication, effective, transition, review, and sunset dates separately, never as a single "date," with the time zone, the treatment of conduct before the effective date, any grandfathering, and what is superseded from when. Evidence: the dated schedule and the rule resolving which version applied to a decision.

### Implementation Ownership

A policy with no implementer is a wish: name who operationalizes it, what must change — process, system, training, forms, contracts — by when, and what authority or resources that needs, and confirm the owner accepted before the effective date. Evidence: the named owner, accepted change list, and dated readiness.

### Monitoring

Decide before adoption how anyone will know the policy is working or harming: the indicator tied to the stated objective, its data source and owner, the observation window, and the threshold that triggers action; compliance activity is not an outcome indicator. The OECD Regulatory Policy Outlook 2025 offers supporting implementation evidence, not a substitute for governing law. Evidence: the monitoring plan recorded before the effective date.

### Review And Revision

Set the review trigger before adoption — a fixed date, a threshold breach, a superior-instrument change, or a sunset — with a named owner and the question it must answer, including whether to withdraw; a policy without a trigger only gets outgrown. Evidence: the recorded trigger, owner, and review question.

## Working Process

1. Establish authority: the instrument or delegation, the scope it reaches, and who holds the decision. Stop at the first decisive ambiguity (see Stop And Escalation).
2. State the problem, the affected parties, and the objective in terms monitoring can observe.
3. Run participation proportionate to consequence and to any mandatory procedure; record each disposition.
4. Assemble evidence: baseline, expected effect, compliance cost, and distributional effects — who gains, who bears the cost.
5. Compare realistic alternatives, including no new instrument, and record why the chosen mechanism fits.
6. Draft, then check against existing instruments, prior interpretations, and superior authority; resolve every conflict by precedence, narrowing, or amendment.
7. Map each requirement to its controlling authority, verification, and exception path, and state what non-conformance triggers.
8. Route the decision to its authorized holder; record the decision, authority, rationale, and rejected alternatives — issuance stays behind its own gate.
9. Fix the date schedule — approval, publication, effective, transition, review, sunset — with time zone, transition handling, and supersession.
10. Confirm implementation ownership and readiness, then publish the controlling version with its identity and change note.
11. Record the monitoring plan and review trigger with owners, then hand off the evidence and open gaps.

## Decision Gates

Answer before proceeding past each stage; a no is a stop or an escalation, not a rationalization.

- What authority makes this binding, and does its scope reach these parties, activities, and period?
- Who decides — and is the decision recorded by that authority's holder rather than the drafter?
- Whose participation is owed, and could the outcome still change when they were consulted?
- Is each claimed effect supported by evidence, and have I named who bears the cost?
- Does this conflict with an existing instrument or prior interpretation, and is each conflict resolved?
- Can a bound reader find the controlling version, tell it from a draft, and read it?
- Which date governs which conduct, and what happens to decisions made before the effective date?
- Who implements this, by when, and have they accepted?
- What will show this working or harming, and what triggers reconsideration — decided now, not later?

## Failure Modes

Each entry pairs the failure with the rationalization that disguises it.

- Missing authority: text that binds without naming what makes it binding — "everyone knows this is the rule." Practice is not delegation.
- Stakeholder theater: consultation run after the decision, or in a form nobody can use — "we posted it for comment." Participation that cannot change the outcome only records one.
- Unsupported impact claim: benefit, cost, or burden asserted with no baseline — "this will obviously reduce risk." It can be neither monitored nor defended.
- Unresolved conflict: a rule contradicting an existing instrument — "the newer one presumably wins." Precedence is recorded by the authority, not assumed by the bound party.
- Ambiguous effective date: one date doing the work of six, with no time zone or transition rule — "it's effective immediately." Someone must still prove which rule applied that week.
- Inaccessible publication: authoritative text the audience cannot obtain, identify, or read — "it's in the shared drive." Publication is measured at the reader.
- Policy without implementation ownership: an adopted rule nobody is resourced to operationalize — "the teams will pick it up." An unowned obligation becomes silent non-compliance.
- No review trigger: adoption with no date, threshold, or event forcing reconsideration — "we'll revisit it if there's a problem." The problem then finds whoever it lands on.

## Evidence And Verification

Produce evidence a reviewer can check without trusting the author: the cited authority with its jurisdiction and scope; the participation record with dispositions; each claimed effect mapped to its supporting fact, including distributional effects; the alternatives compared and the conflict list with dispositions; the requirement-to-authority map with verification and exception paths; the decision record with holder, authority, date, and rationale; the published location and version identity with its audience-reachability check; the separated date schedule with its transition rule; and the implementation owner, monitoring plan, and review trigger with their owners and dates. Approval alone, document length, and consultation volume are never by themselves evidence.

## Examples And Counterexamples

- Interpretive guidance: a one-line clarification inside settled authority that changes no obligation records the authority, the interpretation, the decision holder, and the published version — no impact assessment or consultation round, and the reason each was not required.
- Enforceable policy: a new expense rule with external effect walks the full process — participation with dispositions, cost and distributional evidence, a do-nothing comparison, conflict checks, separated approval, publication, effective and transition dates, an implementation owner, and a review trigger.
- Counterexample: an "immediately effective" rule published only in a chat message — ambiguous dating plus inaccessible publication; a bound reader can neither find the controlling text nor tell which conduct it reached.
- Counterexample: citing the OECD Regulatory Impact Assessment principles as the authority for adopting an internal expense standard — a current source outside its applicability; the organization's own delegation is what makes it bind.
- Counterexample: routing a team README describing how the group prefers to file tickets — ordinary prose and internal preference bind no one.

## Stop And Escalation

Stop and report rather than proceed when: no authority for the binding effect can be named, or the interpretation would exceed that authority's scope; the decision holder is unidentified or has not decided; a mandatory participation procedure cannot be completed; a conflict with an existing instrument or superior authority is unresolved at equal scope; the effective, transition, or supersession rule cannot be stated unambiguously; publication cannot reach the bound audience in a usable form; no implementation owner accepts the change before the effective date; or issuance would occur without its own operator and legal approval. Escalate with the failure-signal grammar: the unverified claim, the missing authority or evidence, the consequence of proceeding, and the decision or proof required. Hand off with the draft state, the authority and decision record, the participation and conflict dispositions, the date schedule, the publication and implementation state, and the review owner.
